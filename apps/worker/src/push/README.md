# Push notifications: the contract

What the worker sends, and what the phone app (M10 step 11) does with it. The lists both sides use live in `@hfos/core` (`packages/core/src/push.ts`): the switches, the categories with their buttons in order, and `PushData`, the schema every push's `data` parses with. The app registers its categories from `PUSH_CATEGORIES` and `PUSH_BUTTONS` rather than copying them.

## How a push travels

- Expo's push service, with **enhanced push security** on: the worker sends with `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN`, which only the worker holds. Apple's key and Firebase's stay in EAS.
- **iPhone:** a normal alert push. `title`, `subtitle` and `body` show on the lock screen, `categoryId` is the category, and `data` carries the rest. `sound: default`; the urgent kinds are `interruptionLevel: time-sensitive` (the app needs the time-sensitive entitlement, or they arrive as ordinary ones).
- **Android:** a **data-only, high-priority** push. Nothing is drawn by the system: the app shows a local notification itself, with the category's buttons, from `data.title`, `data.subtitle` and `data.body`.
- **One notification per conversation:** a push about a conversation carries `data.collapseId` (`conversation-<id>`) and replaces that conversation's earlier notification. On an iPhone it is also the push's `collapseId` and `threadId`; on Android the app gives its local notification that id. A push with `data.quiet: true` is an update: an iPhone takes it in `interruptionLevel: passive` with no sound, and the app on Android replaces its notification without alerting again (only alert once).
- Kept for a phone that's off: 12 hours for a customer's message, a missed call and the urgent kinds, 24 hours for the rest.
- A push is never sent twice to one phone, and a phone that Expo says is gone (`DeviceNotRegistered`) is turned off (`disabled_reason = 'not_registered'`) until the app registers it again.

## Registering a phone

| Command                | When                                                                                             | Input                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `push_device.register` | Signing in, every app start, and a new token                                                     | `{ platform: 'ios' \| 'android', token: 'ExponentPushToken[…]', appVersion, deviceName? }` |
| `push_device.remove`   | Signing out (`reason: 'signed_out'`), or notifications turned off on this phone (`'turned_off'`) | `{ token }` for this phone, or `{ deviceId }` for another of the person's phones           |

Both are for people, can be sent again safely, and are online-only (refused if more than 2 minutes old). Registering takes the token over from anyone who signed in on the phone before. `deviceName` left out keeps the name; `null` clears it. The person's devices sync to the phone without their tokens.

## The switches

`users.settings.push`, one per kind, read with `pushSwitchOn(settings, key)` (the database's `private.push_on`). Each is on unless turned off, except `payment`, which is off until turned on. Save all seven at once: `user.update_profile` with `{ settings: { push: { customerWaiting, missedCall, urgent, approval, booking, assigned, payment } } }` (`pushSwitchesOf(settings)` fills in the defaults). The email switches in `settings.notifications` are separate.

### A customer's new message

On each inbound text, email or website chat, as it arrives:

- **Not while the AI teammate has it:** the conversation is the teammate's, or the teammate is about to answer this message. People hear when it hands the conversation over (its email, then `customer_waiting` if nobody answers).
- **Not for what needs no answer, or already has one:** a STOP, a booking confirmed by reply, a conversation that stays snoozed or closed, a message written more than 15 minutes before it arrived (a mailbox catching up), or a message somebody answered before its push went out.
- **Bursts:** the first message buzzes. More messages in the same conversation within 2 minutes of it, with no reply in between, update that notification with the latest words, quietly (`quiet: true`). After 2 minutes, or after somebody replies, the next message buzzes again.
- `refs.messageId` is the message it is about.

The AI teammate "can take" a conversation when it is on, answers that conversation's channel, and isn't answering it already. Whoever did the thing (booked, assigned, recorded the payment) is never told about it. Paths are the web's, as the emails link: the app maps `/app/w/<ws>/…` to its own screens. Tapping the notification itself opens `data.path`.

Words, as on the canvas: the title names who or what ("Hot lead · Maria Lopez", "Mike Johnson", "Customer waiting · Mike Johnson", "Approval · Angela Brooks", "New booking · Mike Johnson", "Assigned to you · Rosa Delgado", "Paid · Bayou Bistro"); the subtitle names the business, after the channel for a customer's message ("Text · Lone Star HVAC & Roofing"); the body is at most 180 characters. Nothing secret goes in: no links (customer links carry codes), tokens or provider ids.

## What every push carries

`data`, parsed with `PushData`:

| Field                       | What                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `v`                         | `1`                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `notificationId`            | A UUID, one per moment, the same on every phone it reaches                                                                                                                             |
| `category`                  | One of the categories above                                                                                                                                                            |
| `workspaceId`, `business`   | The business, by id and name                                                                                                                                                           |
| `path`                      | Where it opens                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `sentAt`                    | When the moment was queued (ISO)                                                                                                                                                       |
| `refs`                      | The records it is about: `conversationId` and `channel`, `messageId`, `personId`, `leadId`, `itemId`, `approvalId`, `draftId`, `appointmentId`, `taskId`, `invoiceId`, whichever apply |
| `commandIds`                | A UUID for each button that runs a command (`reply`, `let_ai_handle`, `let_handle`, `snooze`, `approve`)                                                                               |
| `collapseId`                | When present: the notification this one replaces (`conversation-<id>`)                                                                                                                 |
| `quiet`                     | When `true`: an update to a notification already showing, shown without a sound or a buzz                                                                                              |
| `title`, `subtitle`, `body` | Android only: the words to show                                                                                                                                                        |

## The buttons

A button that runs a command sends it with the app's usual headers (`x-hfos-client: mobile`, the app version, `x-hfos-occurred-at` as the moment it was pressed) and **`x-hfos-command-id` set to `data.commandIds[button]`**. Pressed twice, or on two of the person's phones, it runs once: the second gets the first answer back. The same id with a different input (a second, different reply) is refused `command_id_reused`; treat that as already done.

| Button                 | Unlock first  | Runs                                                                                                 | Input                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reply                  | Yes           | `message.send`                                                                                       | `{ workspaceId, conversationId, channel: refs.channel, body: <typed> }`, with no `clientId`: one notice reaches several people, and each may reply |
| Call                   | Opens the app | None from the notification: the app opens the customer and offers Call (`call.connect_me`)           | —                                                                                                                                                  |
| Let AI handle          | Yes           | `conversation.hand_to_ai`                                                                            | `{ workspaceId, conversationId }`                                                                                                                  |
| Let HandsFreeOS handle | Yes           | `attention.let_handle`                                                                               | `{ workspaceId, itemId, action: 'hand_to_ai' }`                                                                                                    |
| Snooze 1 hour          | No            | `attention.snooze`                                                                                   | `{ workspaceId, itemId, until: <pressed + PUSH_SNOOZE_MS> }`                                                                                       |
| Approve                | Yes           | `workflow.decide_approval` with `refs.approvalId`, or `ai_teammate.decide_draft` with `refs.draftId` | `{ workspaceId, approvalId \| draftId, decision: 'approve' }`                                                                                      |
| Review, Open           | Opens the app | None                                                                                                 | —                                                                                                                                                  |

"Unlock first" is the button's `unlock` in `PUSH_BUTTONS`. On an iPhone, register the action with `options.opensAppToForeground: false` and `isAuthenticationRequired: true`. Expo's Android code ignores that option, so on Android the app checks the phone is unlocked itself before running one (and reads its session with SecureStore's after-first-unlock setting).

Every button command waits on a phone like the same command from a screen: Reply, Let AI handle, Let HandsFreeOS handle and Approve are fresh for 30 minutes and then ask "Still send?"; Snooze goes any time. A reply that lands in the business's quiet hours is refused with `disposition: ask` (`quiet_hours`): open the conversation and ask, then send again under the same command id.

## Trying it locally

- `PUSH_PROVIDER=fake` is the default outside production. The stand-in keeps every message it was handed. A token with `Gone` in it gets `DeviceNotRegistered` in its receipt; one with `Dead` in it, on its ticket.
- `GET /test/push/sent?workspaceId=<id>` lists what the stand-in was handed for a business: each message's `to`, `platform`, words, `categoryId` and `data`.
- `POST /test/push/run-due { workspaceId }` runs the business's waiting push jobs now, the receipt checks (15 minutes out) included.
- Pushes are queued by the worker's own loop, so read `sent` again until what you expect is there.
