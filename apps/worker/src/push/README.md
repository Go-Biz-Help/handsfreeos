# Push notifications: the contract

What the worker sends, and what the phone app (M10 step 11) does with it. The lists both sides use live in `@hfos/core` (`packages/core/src/push.ts`): the switches, the categories with their buttons in order, and `PushData`, the schema every push's `data` parses with. The app registers its categories from `PUSH_CATEGORIES` and `PUSH_BUTTONS` rather than copying them.

## How a push travels

- Expo's push service, with **enhanced push security** on: the worker sends with `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN`, which only the worker holds. Apple's key and Firebase's stay in EAS.
- **iPhone:** a normal alert push. `title`, `subtitle` and `body` show on the lock screen, `categoryId` is the category, and `data` carries the rest. `sound: default`; the urgent kinds are `interruptionLevel: time-sensitive` (the app needs the time-sensitive entitlement, or they arrive as ordinary ones).
- **Android:** a **data-only, high-priority** push. Nothing is drawn by the system: the app shows a local notification itself, with the category's buttons, from `data.title`, `data.subtitle` and `data.body`.
- **One notification per conversation:** a push about a conversation carries `data.collapseId` (`conversation-<id>`) and replaces that conversation's earlier notification. On an iPhone it is also the push's `collapseId` and `threadId`; on Android the app gives its local notification that id. A push with `data.quiet: true` is an update: an iPhone takes it in `interruptionLevel: passive` with no sound, and the app on Android replaces its notification without alerting again (only alert once).
- Kept for a phone that's off: 12 hours for a customer's message, a missed call, the urgent kinds, a visit to mark and a new support ticket; 24 hours for the rest.
- A push is never sent twice to one phone, and a phone that Expo says is gone (`DeviceNotRegistered`) is turned off (`disabled_reason = 'not_registered'`) until the app registers it again.

## Registering a phone

| Command                | When                                                                                             | Input                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `push_device.register` | Signing in, every app start, and a new token                                                     | `{ platform: 'ios' \| 'android', token: 'ExponentPushToken[…]', appVersion, deviceName? }` |
| `push_device.remove`   | Signing out (`reason: 'signed_out'`), or notifications turned off on this phone (`'turned_off'`) | `{ token }` for this phone, or `{ deviceId }` for another of the person's phones           |

Both are for people, can be sent again safely, and are online-only (refused if more than 2 minutes old). Registering takes the token over from anyone who signed in on the phone before. `deviceName` left out keeps the name; `null` clears it. The person's devices sync to the phone without their tokens.

## The switches

`users.settings.push`, one per kind, read with `pushSwitchOn(settings, key)` (the database's `private.push_on`, which refuses a name it doesn't know). Each is on unless turned off, except `payment`, which is off until turned on. Save all thirteen at once: `user.update_profile` with `{ settings: { push: { customerWaiting, missedCall, urgent, approval, booking, assigned, payment, wallet, jobs, connections, addons, supportTickets, qualifying } } }` (`pushSwitchesOf(settings)` fills in the defaults, in `PushSwitches`' order). The email switches in `settings.notifications` are separate. `addons` is for an add-on's alerts (Real Estate step 5), with its email switch `addonEmail`.

`qualifying` (20261018000400) is the AI teammate's qualifying, to the people its settings name: a lead qualifies, or a customer sends the file a question asks for. Its row shows to everyone; an older app build that saves fewer switches leaves it on.

`safety` isn't a switch anyone sees or saves: a check-in's pushes always go (`private.push_on` answers true), since a missed one is a false alarm or nobody told.

`supportTickets` is for HandsFreeOS's own support people. Settings → Notifications shows its row, New support tickets, only to someone `platform.me` says answers support (the web's Settings → Account and the phone's alike); everyone's save carries it anyway, since a save sends every switch.

### An extension sign-in

Someone typed the person's email in the Social Outreach extension and pressed **Send me a prompt**
(20261027002910, apps/extension/README.md "Signing in with a prompt"):

- **`extension_sign_in`** ("Signing in to the extension on Chrome?") to that person's own phones alone, at once, with
  **Open**. It belongs to no business: `workspaceId: null`, `business: ''`, `path: /app/extension-sign-in/<id>`,
  `refs.signInId`, `collapseId: sign-in-<id>`. Never the number itself. Time-sensitive, kept 2 minutes (the request's
  life), and not a switch: only the person can have asked for it.
- **How the worker sends it:** queued by the worker's public start door in the transaction that makes the request
  (`queuePersonalPush`, `push:extension_sign_in:<id>`), in a savepoint, only when the email has an account with a phone
  on. Its phones, and turning off the ones Expo says are gone, go through `private.own_push_devices` and
  `private.turn_off_own_push_devices`, the worker's alone.
- **On the phone:** Open, or a tap, opens the screen that asks for the number (`app/app/extension-sign-in/[requestId]`),
  which reads `extension_sign_in.pending` and answers with `extension_sign_in.answer`. Shown (Android) to whoever is
  signed in on the phone, since it only ever goes to that person's own phones.
- **Older app builds** don't know the kind, and its path doesn't parse for them: Android shows nothing, an iPhone shows
  it with no Open button. The web's prompt and the emailed code still work.

### A missed check-out (round six)

A person checked in somewhere alone (`safety.check_in`) and didn't check out by the planned end (apps/worker/src/safety/ladder.ts):

- **`safety_check`** ("Still at 2207 Juniper Trail?") to the person alone, at the planned end plus the business's "ask after" minutes, with **I’m OK** (`safety.check_out`) and **+15 min** (`safety.extend`, 15 minutes). Neither needs an unlock: whoever holds the phone is the one asked. Lives an hour (`PUSH_TTL_SECONDS`) and is time-sensitive. The phone sets the same push for itself when the person checks in or adds time (`apps/mobile/src/safety/actions.ts`), under the same notification id, with the words read from its own copy: the business's time zone and times, and the contacts who'd be told. So the person is asked with no signal, and pressing a button with none checks out or moves that reminder on the phone.
- **`safety_alert`** to each teammate they named as a contact, with nobody answering the business's "tell after" minutes later, then every ten minutes for an hour; with no contact who'd hear, the business's safety person, else its owners and admins. Open shows the alert (`/app/w/<ws>/safety/<checkInId>`). "<Name> is OK" follows on the same notification (`collapseId: safety-<checkInId>`) once they check out, add time or someone reaches them.
- Both carry `refs.checkInId`, and never more than the place, the planned end and the person's phone: the words are core's (`safetyAskWords`, `safetyAlertPushWords`), the same the phone shows.

Someone outside is told by text and email instead, never a push, and only after agreeing by text (Reply OK).

### A customer's new message

On each inbound text, email or website chat, as it arrives:

- **Not while the AI teammate has it:** the conversation is the teammate's, or the teammate is about to answer this message. People hear when it hands the conversation over (its email, then `customer_waiting` if nobody answers).
- **Not for what needs no answer, or already has one:** a STOP, a booking confirmed by reply, a conversation that stays snoozed or closed, a message written more than 15 minutes before it arrived (a mailbox catching up), or a message somebody answered before its push went out.
- **Bursts:** the first message buzzes. More messages in the same conversation within 2 minutes of it, with no reply in between, update that notification with the latest words, quietly (`quiet: true`). After 2 minutes, or after somebody replies, the next message buzzes again.
- `refs.messageId` is the message it is about.

**Someone's own mail (M19)** is a `customer_message_no_ai` too, on the same switch, since its Reply and Call work on it and every app build knows the category. It goes to that person alone, when new mail lands in their own Inbox (`email.filed` with `private: true`). Their own mail never waits on a reply, so there is no burst: each message replaces the thread's last notification. The team's Other mail and Spam push nobody.

### A new support ticket

HandsFreeOS's own support desk tells its support people on their phones when someone new needs them. It is the one kind of push that belongs to no business.

- **When:** a ticket starts, either way in. Asking from inside the app (`support.request.create`, its `support_ticket.opened` event), or mail to support@ that starts something new (the worker's support mail, which files it directly). A customer's answer on a ticket that's already there, reopening it or not, pushes nobody: the desk's queue shows it.
- **Who:** every active support person (`platform.staff`, not suspended, account not deleted) with `supportTickets` on and a phone on, except a support person who wrote in themselves. Nobody's business matters, and nobody in a business hears of it.
- **Words:** "New support ticket #1042"; the subtitle is who wrote and the business it's about when known ("Dana Reed · Reed Plumbing", or their address); the body is the subject and the start of what they wrote ("Reminders stopped going out: “They went out on Monday and not since.”"). `supportTicketPushWords` in core makes them.
- **What it carries:** `workspaceId: null`, `business: ''`, `path: /admin/tickets/<ticketId>`, `refs.ticketId`, `collapseId: ticket-<ticketId>`, and no command ids: Open, and a tap, open the ticket on the phone's Support desk (the route map sends `/admin/…` to `/app/admin/…`, which asks the API whether the person answers support).
- **How the worker sends it:** queued in the transaction that files the ticket (`queueStaffPush`, one job per ticket, `push:support_ticket:<ticketId>`), in a savepoint, so a push that can't be queued never costs the ticket. The job holds no business. Its phones, and turning off the ones Expo says are gone, go through two functions only the worker may call, `private.staff_push_devices` and `private.turn_off_staff_push_devices`: from a business's scope the worker sees only that business's people's phones. Everything else is the business path's: sent once per phone, 100 to a request, the switch checked again as it sends.
- **On the phone:** shown (Android) only when the person signed in answers support, from the answer to `platform.me` the app keeps (asked as it opens). A support push naming a business, or a business's push opening the desk, doesn't parse.
- **Older app builds** don't know the kind, and a push with no business doesn't parse for them: Android shows nothing, and an iPhone shows it (iOS draws it) with no Open button, and a tap only opens the app. `v` stays `1`: nothing a build already reads has changed, and a new version would have made them drop every push. A support person needs a build that has it.

### Where each opens, and what it says

The AI teammate "can take" a conversation when it is on, answers that conversation's channel, and isn't answering it already. Whoever did the thing (booked, assigned, recorded the payment) is never told about it. Paths are the web's, as the emails link: the app maps `/app/w/<ws>/…` to its own screens, and the support desk's `/admin/…` to its Support desk. Tapping the notification itself opens `data.path`.

Words, as on the canvas: the title names who or what ("Hot lead · Maria Lopez", "Mike Johnson", "Customer waiting · Mike Johnson", "Approval · Angela Brooks", "New booking · Mike Johnson", "Assigned to you · Rosa Delgado", "Paid · Bayou Bistro", "File sent · Rosa Diaz", "Qualified · Rosa Diaz", "New support ticket #1042"); the subtitle names the business, after the channel for a customer's message ("Text · Lone Star HVAC & Roofing"), or, for a new support ticket, who wrote and their business; the body is at most 180 characters. Nothing secret goes in: no links (customer links carry codes), tokens or provider ids.

## The buttons

A button that runs a command sends it with the app's usual headers (`x-hfos-client: mobile`, the app version, `x-hfos-occurred-at` as the moment it was pressed) and **`x-hfos-command-id` set to `data.commandIds[button]`**. Pressed twice, or on two of the person's phones, it runs once: the second gets the first answer back. The same id with a different input (a second, different reply) is refused `command_id_reused`; treat that as already done.

| Button                 | Unlock first  | Runs                                                                                                 | Input                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reply                  | Yes           | `message.send`                                                                                       | `{ workspaceId, conversationId, channel: refs.social ?? refs.channel, body: <typed> }`, with no `clientId`: one notice reaches several people, and each may reply |
| Call                   | Opens the app | None from the notification: the app opens the customer and offers Call (`call.connect_me`)           | —                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Let AI handle          | Yes           | `conversation.hand_to_ai`                                                                            | `{ workspaceId, conversationId }`                                                                                                                                 |
| Let HandsFreeOS handle | Yes           | `attention.let_handle`                                                                               | `{ workspaceId, itemId, action: 'hand_to_ai' }`                                                                                                                   |
| Snooze 1 hour          | No            | `attention.snooze`                                                                                   | `{ workspaceId, itemId, until: <pressed + PUSH_SNOOZE_MS> }`                                                                                                      |
| Approve                | Yes           | `workflow.decide_approval` with `refs.approvalId`, or `ai_teammate.decide_draft` with `refs.draftId` | `{ workspaceId, approvalId \| draftId, decision: 'approve' }`                                                                                                     |
| Review, Open           | Opens the app | None                                                                                                 | —                                                                                                                                                                 |

"Unlock first" is the button's `unlock` in `PUSH_BUTTONS`. On an iPhone, register the action with `options.opensAppToForeground: false` and `isAuthenticationRequired: true`. Expo's Android code ignores that option, so on Android the app checks the phone is unlocked itself before running one (and reads its session with SecureStore's after-first-unlock setting).

Every button command waits on a phone like the same command from a screen: Reply, Let AI handle, Let HandsFreeOS handle and Approve are fresh for 30 minutes and then ask "Still send?"; Snooze goes any time. A reply that lands in the business's quiet hours is refused with `disposition: ask` (`quiet_hours`): open the conversation and ask, then send again under the same command id.

## Under the bell

Every push is also kept for the person it reached (20261027012600), so the bell lists what came, newest first: at the top right of the phone app, and on the web at the top of the side menu (beside Ask HandsFreeOS at phone width). The words the bell uses are `BELL_WORDS` in `packages/core/src/bell.ts`.

- **Only the business on screen** (the owner's pick): a business's bell lists and counts that business's pushes, never another's, so switching business switches the bell. Pushes about no business (a new support ticket, a sign-in to the extension) are the person's own and show in every business's bell.

- **One line per person per thing:** a row in `app.notifications` keyed on the push's `collapseId` (a conversation, a support ticket), or on the push itself when it replaces nothing. The send job keeps it (`private.keep_push_notification`) once a phone was handed the push, or may have been (a lost answer); a push every phone refused isn't kept. A newer push about the same thing takes the line over: its words, at the top, new again. The same push again, or an older one that went late, changes nothing. Only people who can still open the business get one.
- **Its words are the push's:** title, subtitle (it names the business), body, and `data.path`, where pressing the line opens, as tapping the push does. `refs` are kept too, so an add-on alert's push (`refs.alertId`) and the alert itself count once.
- **Kept 30 days.** The nightly `notifications.clean_up` job (`private.delete_old_notifications`, beside the receipts' clean-up) removes older ones, and a support person's desk notifications once they no longer answer support. Losing access to a business removes that business's; deleting an account removes them all.
- **Reading them:** `notification.list` (`{ workspaceId, limit?, cursor? }`) gives the person's own in that business, and about no business, newest first, a page at a time, with how many are `unseen`. The phone syncs all of them in `me_v1` (`notifications`), so its bell works offline, and lists the business on screen's the same way.
- **Seen and read:** opening the bell sends `notification.seen { workspaceId, upTo }`, the newest time it showed, which clears that business's count and leaves every other business's. Pressing a line sends `notification.read { ids }`, and tapping the push itself `notification.read { pushIds: [data.notificationId] }`; a line stays marked "Not opened yet" until then. Both are the person's own: online only, no event, no receipt. The app and the web show them at once, before HandsFreeOS answers.
- **Live on the web:** keeping one broadcasts `notification.kept` on the business's channel, so a page open in that business refreshes its bell. One about no business shows within a minute, or when the tab comes back.
- **The add-ons' alerts** still open in the business sit at the top of the bell, under Needs you, until they're done.

## Trying it locally

- `PUSH_PROVIDER=fake` is the default outside production. The stand-in keeps every message it was handed. A token with `Gone` in it gets `DeviceNotRegistered` in its receipt; one with `Dead` in it, on its ticket.
- `GET /test/push/sent?workspaceId=<id>` lists what the stand-in was handed for a business: each message's `to`, `platform`, words, `categoryId` and `data`. A new support ticket's push has no business, so it isn't listed there; the worker's own tests read the stand-in (`apps/worker/test/support-push.test.ts`).
- `POST /test/push/run-due { workspaceId }` runs the business's waiting push jobs now, the receipt checks (15 minutes out) included.
- Pushes are queued by the worker's own loop, so read `sent` again until what you expect is there.
